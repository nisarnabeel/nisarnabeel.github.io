Srinagar Itinerary

One Day in Srinagar: A Complete Itinerary

This is a genuinely full day, not a relaxed one. It covers Dal Lake, two Mughal gardens, a hilltop temple, a shrine, the old city's grand mosque, and real places to eat along the way. If you only have one day in Srinagar, this is how to use it well.

Where to Stay First

Where you sleep shapes the whole day. A hotel or houseboat right on Dal Lake puts you closest to the morning's first stop and gives you the classic Srinagar view, but it comes at a real premium, lake-facing rooms are the most expensive in the city. Staying in the city center around Lal Chowk or Residency Road is noticeably cheaper and still puts you within a short drive of everything below. Either way, use the Restaurants Near Your Hotel tool once you've booked to see what's actually close to where you're staying.

The Day at a Glance

  1. Morning: Shikara ride on Dal Lake
  2. Late morning: Nishat Bagh and Shalimar Bagh (or the Tulip Garden in spring)
  3. Midday: Lunch near the gardens
  4. Afternoon: Pari Mahal and Shankaracharya Temple
  5. Evening: Hazratbal, Jamia Masjid, and Lal Chowk
  6. Dinner: Wazwan
Morning, 7:00 to 9:00 AM

Start on the Water

Shikara rides run daily from 6 AM to 6 PM, but the morning is genuinely the best time: the lake is calm, it's far less crowded, and the light is better for photos than the touristy midday rush. A short ride is 30 to 60 minutes; a longer one covering the floating gardens and Char Chinar island runs 1 to 2 hours. Expect to pay around ₹700 to ₹800 per hour for a standard shikara, and always agree on the price before boarding, bargaining is normal here, especially in peak season.

Dal Lake

4.6★ · 10,827 reviews

The centerpiece of Srinagar and where nearly every visit starts. Beyond the shikara ride itself, this is also where the houseboats, floating vegetable market, and Mughal-era lakeside gardens are all clustered, so getting oriented here early sets up the rest of the day.

Breakfast: Grab kahwa and something light before or after the ride. Mayday Cafe and Bistro near Lal Chowk (4.9★, 210 reviews) is specifically known for kahwa if you're not eating at your hotel.

Late Morning, 9:30 AM to 12:00 PM

The Mughal Gardens

Nishat Bagh and Shalimar Bagh are both open 9 AM to 7 PM daily, entry is a token fee (under ₹30 for Indian visitors at each). If you're visiting in spring, specifically mid-March to mid-April, swap one of these for the Tulip Garden instead, it's only open for about a month a year and is genuinely worth prioritizing over a second Mughal garden while it's in bloom.

Nishat Bagh

4.4★ · 20,681 reviews

A terraced Mughal garden facing Dal Lake with the Zabarwan mountains behind it. Twelve terraces step down toward the water, each representing a sign of the zodiac in the original 17th-century design.

Open 9 AM–7 PM daily. Entry roughly ₹20 for Indian visitors.

Shalimar Bagh

4.4★ · 22,332 reviews

Built by Emperor Jahangir for his wife Nur Jahan, and the most famous of Srinagar's Mughal gardens. The black marble pavilion at the top terrace was once reserved for the emperor and his guests alone.

Open 9 AM–7 PM daily. Entry roughly ₹24 for Indian visitors.

Indira Gandhi Memorial Tulip Garden (spring only)

4.5★ · 17,696 reviews

Asia's largest tulip garden, around 1.8 million bulbs across more than 70 varieties spread over seven terraces. It only opens for roughly a month, typically mid-March to mid-April, so this only belongs on the itinerary if your visit lands in that window.

Entry roughly ₹75 for Indian adults, ₹200 for foreign visitors. A separate camera fee applies for professional equipment.

Midday, 12:30 to 1:30 PM

Lunch Near the Gardens

Lunch: Both Shahi Dawat by Lake Avenue (4.9★, 347 reviews, known for rista) and SALAM E WAZWAN (4.3★, 1,980 reviews) sit close to this part of the lake, both good options if you want a proper Kashmiri meal without doubling back into the city center.

Afternoon, 2:00 to 4:30 PM

Up the Hills

Pari Mahal

4.4★ · 9,113 reviews

The "Palace of Fairies," built in the mid-1600s by Dara Shikoh, Shah Jahan's eldest son. It's believed to sit on the site of an earlier Buddhist monastery and was later used as an observatory for teaching astronomy. Now it's a terraced garden with sweeping views over the lake, and one of the quieter stops on this list.

Open 9:30 AM–5:30 PM, closed Fridays. Entry roughly ₹20–24 for Indian visitors.

Shankaracharya Temple

4.8★ · 9,460 reviews

A hilltop temple dedicated to Shiva, and the single best panoramic view of Srinagar and Dal Lake together. There's a real security process here, expect an ID check on the road up and a checkpoint at the gate where phones and cameras get deposited before you climb the final steps. Dress to cover your shoulders and knees.

Security check at the gate; electronics aren't allowed past it. Budget extra time for this compared to the other stops.

Evening, 5:00 to 7:30 PM

The Old City and the Bazaar

Dargah Hazratbal

4.7★ · 3,276 reviews

A white marble shrine on the northern shore of Dal Lake, believed to hold a relic of the Prophet Muhammad. Visitors of all faiths are welcome; cover your head and dress modestly, gents and boys can borrow a skull cap at the entrance if needed.

Jamia Masjid

4.7★ · 6,312 reviews

Srinagar's grand mosque in the heart of the old city, built around 370 wooden pillars in a style that owes more to Central Asian architecture than typical South Asian mosques. Leave your footwear at the entrance, keep your head covered, and note that women are generally directed to the outer courtyard rather than the main prayer hall.

Lal Chowk and Saffron Shopping

Srinagar's city center, good for an evening walk and for picking up Kashmiri saffron before you leave. Buy from an established shop rather than a street stall, real Kashmiri saffron (kesar) is genuinely expensive and commonly cut or substituted by less scrupulous sellers, so a shop with a track record and real reviews is worth the extra trust.

Dinner, 8:00 PM onward

Close With Wazwan

End the day the way most Kashmiri celebrations do. See the full Where to Get Real Wazwan guide for ranked picks with real ratings, but Ahdoos and Kareema are both in the Regal Chowk area, an easy stop if you're winding down near the city center after Lal Chowk.

Being realistic about pacing: this covers a lot of ground in one day, and Srinagar traffic plus walking time between stops adds up fast. If you're short on time, the shikara ride, one Mughal garden, and Wazwan for dinner is a strong minimum. Shankaracharya Temple takes longer than it looks because of the security process, budget for that specifically if you're keeping it in.
