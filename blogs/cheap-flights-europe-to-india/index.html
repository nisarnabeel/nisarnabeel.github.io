Cheap Flights from Europe to India: IndiGo vs Air India vs Lufthansa

I fly this route often enough to have real opinions on it. Here's an honest look at the main ways to get from Europe to India, what each airline actually gets you, and why "cheapest" and "best" aren't the same flight.

Based on real routes, real flight numbers, and cross-checked reviews, not a travel agency's marketing copy.

In this guide

  1. IndiGo: the new long-haul contender
  2. Air India: direct, but the aircraft matters
  3. Lufthansa: expensive, but the most reliable
  4. KLM and Air France: solid middle ground
  5. The honest verdict

There are really three ways to fly Europe to India: a direct flight on an Indian carrier (IndiGo or Air India), a one-stop flight on a European legacy carrier through their home hub (Lufthansa via Frankfurt/Munich, KLM via Amsterdam, Air France via Paris), or a budget routing through a Central Asian or Gulf hub, which is usually the cheapest option but adds real time and an extra layover. This is about the first two, since those are what most people flying from Amsterdam, Paris, Frankfurt, or Munich actually end up comparing.

Usually cheapest

IndiGo

IndiGo has been aggressively expanding into long-haul Europe routes, and pricing is usually the most competitive of the group, with one-way fares showing up as low as roughly ₹12,000-13,000 in off-peak windows. Amsterdam to Mumbai runs as flight 6E22 (6E021 on the return), worth knowing since IndiGo's Amsterdam service connects to Mumbai, not Delhi, so factor in a domestic leg if Delhi or further north is your actual destination.

As of this week, that route is genuinely flying a real widebody: a Boeing 787-9 Dreamliner, wet-leased from Norse Atlantic Airways, running daily with a consistent 9-10 hour block time (verified against live flight history, not schedule data). That said, this arrangement has a shelf life. IndiGo confirmed in August 2026 that it's winding the Norse Atlantic 787 lease down more broadly, and its Delhi-London Heathrow 787 service specifically pauses from 25 October 2026, with widebody service not fully returning until IndiGo's own Airbus A350s arrive in 2027. In the interim, expect some Europe routes to shift toward the Airbus A321XLR, a narrowbody built for long, thin long-haul routes, so double-check the aircraft type for your specific dates before booking.

Two things to budget for if you fly IndiGo internationally: meals are typically included on long-haul international fares, but comfort extras like a blanket, pillow, and eye shade are a separate paid add-on you pre-book through "Manage My Booking," not something handed out for free the way it is on legacy carriers.

Direct, but inconsistent

Air India

Air India runs true direct flights, Amsterdam to Delhi is flight AI156 (AI155 on the return), operated on a Boeing 787-8 Dreamliner, roughly 9 hours nonstop. That directness is the real appeal: no layover, no connection risk.

The catch, and this shows up repeatedly in independent reviews, not just word of mouth, is aircraft condition varies a lot depending on which specific 787 you get. Multiple Tripadvisor reviews of Air India's Amsterdam-Delhi route describe broken or barely-functional entertainment screens and worn interiors on the older jets in the fleet. Air India's newer 787-9s reportedly have a genuinely upgraded system with sharp 4K screens, but you don't get to choose which aircraft shows up for your flight. If in-flight entertainment actually working matters to you, treat it as a coin flip on this specific route rather than a guarantee.

Most reliable, most expensive

Lufthansa

Lufthansa flies from Frankfurt and Munich to Delhi, Mumbai, Bengaluru, and Chennai, the widest India network of any European carrier, typically with a short connection through its home hub rather than a long layover. Frankfurt to Delhi runs as flight LH760 (LH761 on the return). It's consistently the most expensive of the group, but the tradeoff is real: newer aircraft on the India routes, a more predictable service standard, and none of the "which specific plane will I get" uncertainty that comes with Air India. If your dates are flexible, Lufthansa fares are usually cheapest booked 2-4 months out.

KLM and Air France

KLM flies Amsterdam to Delhi as flight KL871 (KL872 on the return), plus Mumbai and Bengaluru, and Air France flies Paris to the same three cities, Paris to Delhi runs as AF226 (Air France rotates a few flight numbers on this route, including AF214 and AF284 depending on the day). Both are Air India's actual codeshare/alliance partners on parts of this corridor. Neither is usually the cheapest option, but both sit in a reasonable middle ground: better aircraft consistency than Air India, without quite Lufthansa's price premium. If you're flying from Amsterdam specifically and want a one-stop option instead of IndiGo or Air India's direct flight, KLM is the natural comparison to check.

On timing: January, February, and early November are consistently the cheapest windows to fly this route, with European winter fares running roughly 35-45% lower than peak summer (June-August). If your travel dates are flexible at all, that matters more than which airline you pick.

The Honest Verdict

  • Cheapest, and you don't mind a narrowbody long-haul cabin: IndiGo. Just know you won't get the space or extras of a true widebody during the current A321XLR-heavy period.
  • Want the shortest routing (direct, no layover) and can tolerate some unpredictability: Air India. Real chance of a tired cabin and a dead entertainment screen, balanced against saving a connection.
  • Willing to pay more for consistency: Lufthansa. The most expensive of the three, and also the one with the least "which plane am I getting" anxiety.
  • Want a middle ground with more route choice than IndiGo/Air India but less cost than Lufthansa: KLM or Air France, depending on whether Amsterdam or Paris works better for you.
